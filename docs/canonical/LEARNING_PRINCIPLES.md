# Learning Principles

## The unit of progress

The fundamental unit is not a lesson or a day. It is a capability moving through states such as:

- new;
- recognized;
- active;
- automatic.

Lexical knowledge, construction knowledge, contrast selection, listening, communicative functions, pronunciation, and transfer are tracked separately where useful.

## Voice learning loop

For new or weak material, the preferred progression is:

**Expose → Understand → Imitate → Retrieve → Manipulate → Embed → Surprise-test → Recycle**

Existing material can enter later in the loop.

## Practice modes

The engine may use:

1. **Acquisition** — concise introduction, pinyin/English allowed.
2. **Retrieval** — learner produces from meaning/context; answer withheld.
3. **Manipulation** — vary subject, negation, tense/aspect, object, question, connector, etc.
4. **Simulation** — model adopts a role; learner must accomplish a goal.
5. **Free conversation** — unscripted conversation with subtle elicitation of targets.
6. **Listening** — learner proves meaning through an appropriate response rather than constant translation.
7. **Testing** — no pinyin/answer hints; novel prompts and delayed retrieval.

## Correction policy

Correct almost everything, but spend time proportional to the error.

- Minor/naturalness issue: model briefly; at most one retry; continue.
- Meaningful grammar/lexical/pronunciation issue: stop, identify briefly, model, retry, continue.
- Repeated/systematic issue: short contrast/drill intervention, novel test, return to conversation.
- Communication breakdown: learner attempts repair/paraphrase before the model supplies the answer.

Never “correct” valid Mandarin merely because another phrasing is preferred. Optional natural upgrades may be mentioned briefly.

## Hint ladder

When the learner is stuck:

0. Allow silence/retrieval time.
1. Context/meaning cue.
2. Partial cue.
3. Full pinyin target.
4. Model phrase/frame.
5. Full answer.

Hint level is evidence.

## Listening ladder

- **L1:** deliberately clear, somewhat slower, still natural Mandarin.
- **L2:** cooperative natural speech from a native speaker who knows the listener is learning.
- **L3:** ordinary clear native conversational speed.
- Later bands introduce faster/reduced/uncontrolled input.

Band 1 must not graduate on L1-only comprehension.

## Controlled language

During early controlled practice, most input should consist of known/assigned language. A useful heuristic is approximately 80–90% known material and 10–20% new/challenging material, not a rigid numeric rule.

New vocabulary types:

- **Curriculum new:** intentionally being acquired.
- **Incidental useful:** model may suggest for later orchestrator review.
- **Incidental ignore:** conversation-specific; does not enter curriculum.

## Repetition and interleaving

New targets should recur after increasing interference/delay and across different contexts, not only in massed repetition.

Prefer interleaved selection and contrast drills, especially where confusion is predictable.

## Tutor speech economy

The learner should do most of the cognitive and spoken work.

Default tutor behaviour:

- one short teaching action or question per turn;
- no process narration such as “let me think”;
- minimal motivational chatter;
- no unnecessary translation of the learner’s own Mandarin;
- longer explanations only for repeated/systematic problems or explicit requests.
