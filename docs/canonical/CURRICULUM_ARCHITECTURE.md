# Curriculum Architecture

## Overall progression

The conceptual spoken-Mandarin progression is:

1. **Control** — keep simple interactions alive.
2. **Survive** — complete everyday transactions independently.
3. **Coordinate** — arrange real life, plans, times, and changes.
4. **Socialise** — sustain ordinary informal interaction.
5. **Narrate** — tell coherent stories and recount events.
6. **Explain** — explain ideas, problems, reasons, and processes.
7. **Discuss** — sustain substantive conversation and compare perspectives.
8. **Operate** — function independently in Mandarin across normal life.

Band counts are capability bands, not calendar periods.

## Band 1 — Control

Band 1 mastery means the learner can keep a simple Mandarin interaction alive, communicate immediate wants/needs/preferences, ask basic questions, operate with simple time/quantity/location, and repair misunderstandings without immediate English escape.

### Canonical Band 1 content

- 150 Core lexemes.
- 125 Wider lexemes.
- 60 productive construction families.
- 12 contrast sets.
- 8 closed/generative systems.
- 25 communicative functions.
- 10 scenario environments, each with graded variants.
- 12 pronunciation competencies.
- automatic repair/discourse chunks.
- 7 assessment modules plus hard graduation gates.

The canonical dataset, not prose summaries, is authoritative for the exact item IDs and wording.

## Priority logic

### Core-A

Approximately the highest-value ~75 lexical items. These receive extreme automaticity pressure.

### Core-B

Remaining Core items. Mandatory and heavily practised, but less valuable than Core-A.

### Wider-W1

High-value real-world breadth that should become productive.

### Wider-W2

Useful situational breadth; listening recognition may precede full automatic production.

## Contrast sets are first-class

Knowing items independently does not prove correct selection. Band 1 includes explicit contrast objects such as:

- `bù / méi / méiyǒu`;
- `yào / xiǎng / xūyào`;
- `huì / néng / kěyǐ`;
- `zhīdào / rènshi`;
- `dǒng / míngbai / tīngdǒng`;
- question/space/demonstrative contrasts;
- numeric `yī / èr / liǎng`;
- pinyin-homophone distinctions such as different `zài`, `kuài`, `shì`, `zuò`, `jìn`, and `yào` records.

## Closed systems

Some systems are generative and should not consume arbitrary vocabulary slots, including numbers, phone numbers, money, clock time, calendar, classifiers, relative time, and sequencing.

## Band 1 scenarios

Canonical environments include:

- meeting someone;
- café;
- restaurant;
- convenience store/payment;
- taxi/ride;
- metro/station/directions;
- apartment/home;
- work/university;
- meeting a friend;
- pharmacy/basic health.

Scenarios are testing/practice environments, not memorised lessons. Transfer variants deliberately use untrained settings.

## Band 1 graduation philosophy

Do not average away fundamental weaknesses. Hard gates include:

- conversation repair;
- core question/negation/want/location/time machinery;
- meaningful L2 and emerging L3 listening;
- novel transfer;
- pronunciation intelligibility.

A learner with high flashcard scores and poor real-time listening/repair does not pass.
